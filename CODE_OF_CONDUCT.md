# Code of Conduct — Stack Underflow

## Our Pledge

Stack Underflow is an open source educational Q&A platform for university students. We want everyone to feel safe and welcome when asking questions, sharing knowledge, reporting bugs or contributing to the project.

We are committed to a community free from harassment and discrimination, regardless of age, disability, ethnicity, nationality, race, religion, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, personal appearance, language, educational background or level of technical experience.

Everyone should have an equal opportunity to participate and learn. Beginners are as welcome as experienced developers.

## Our Standards

### Expected behavior

- Use respectful, welcoming and inclusive language.
- Respect different opinions, cultures and levels of experience.
- Give constructive feedback and explain technical decisions clearly.
- Help beginners without judging their knowledge or mistakes.
- Accept constructive criticism and acknowledge mistakes.
- Respect other people's privacy and intellectual property.
- Keep discussions relevant to the project and its educational purpose.

### Unacceptable behavior

- Bullying, threats, harassment, insults or personal attacks.
- Discrimination, hate speech or derogatory comments.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Spam, trolling, impersonation or intentionally disruptive behavior.
- Sharing passwords, access tokens, private keys or other sensitive information.
- Posting malicious content or encouraging harm to other users.
- Retaliating against someone who reports a concern in good faith.

## Rules for Questions and Comments

Stack Underflow allows users to publish questions, tags and comments. To keep the platform useful and safe:

- Write clear questions and use relevant tags.
- Discuss the technical problem rather than attacking the person asking it.
- Provide helpful answers and acknowledge external sources when appropriate.
- Remove personal information and credentials from code examples and screenshots before publishing them.
- Do not use questions or comments to advertise unrelated services, harass users or distribute harmful content.

These rules describe expected community behavior. They do not imply that automatic moderation or an in-app reporting system has been implemented.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to:

- The Stack Underflow application, including questions, tags and comments.
- The GitHub repository, issues, pull requests and code reviews.
- Project-related discussions and communication channels.
- Situations where a participant officially represents the project.

The same standards apply to users, contributors and maintainers.

## Maintainer Responsibilities

Stack Underflow is maintained by **Boris Dubovoy** and **Adil Yergazy**.

The maintainers are responsible for explaining community standards, reviewing reports fairly, protecting confidential information and taking proportionate action when necessary. They may remove harmful content, close discussions, reject contributions or restrict participation where they have the necessary permissions.

Maintainers must also follow this Code of Conduct. If a report concerns one maintainer, it should be handled by the other whenever possible.

## Reporting a Problem

Report harassment, discrimination or other serious concerns privately to a maintainer using an available private contact method. Do not publish sensitive reports in a public GitHub issue.

A dedicated private reporting address has not yet been established. If you do not have a private contact method, you may open a neutral GitHub issue requesting one, without including names of affected users, evidence or details of the incident.

When reporting privately, include only the information needed to review the incident:

- A short description of what happened.
- The relevant question, comment, issue or pull request link, if available.
- The date and any relevant evidence, with unnecessary personal information removed.

Ordinary technical bugs can be reported through [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/Adul23/stack-underflow/issues). See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) for contribution guidelines and [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) for information about project data.

## Enforcement Process

The proposed community moderation procedure is:

1. **Receive:** A maintainer receives the report and acknowledges it when possible.
2. **Review:** The maintainer reviews the available information fairly and avoids unnecessary disclosure.
3. **Decide:** The maintainer selects an appropriate response based on the severity and frequency of the behavior.
4. **Communicate:** Relevant participants are informed privately when appropriate.
5. **Follow up:** The maintainers review repeated violations or new evidence and take further action if needed.

Reports should be handled as confidentially as possible. Information should only be shared when necessary to review the concern or protect the community.

## Enforcement Guidelines

| Level | Example | Possible response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Correction | Minor inappropriate language or a first-time mistake | Private feedback and a request to correct the behavior |
| Warning | Repeated disruption or disrespectful behavior | Formal warning and removal of harmful content where possible |
| Temporary restriction | Serious or repeated harassment | Temporary restriction from relevant project spaces where supported |
| Permanent restriction | Severe threats, persistent harassment or repeated serious violations | Permanent restriction from relevant project spaces where supported |

These are possible responses, not automatic penalties. Serious incidents may require immediate action. Participants may request a review of a decision privately and provide additional context.

## Non-Discrimination and Do No Harm

This policy supports the Digital Public Goods (DPG) principle of Non-Discrimination and Do No Harm. Stack Underflow aims to provide an inclusive educational environment, protect users from harmful behavior and encourage responsible sharing of knowledge.

This policy is a community commitment, not proof of formal DPG certification or fully implemented moderation tools. The maintainers should review it as the project develops.
